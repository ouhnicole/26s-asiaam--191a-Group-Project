About this Project

Authors: Cathy Luong and Nicole Ouh

Background

Background

In 2025, the federal government introduced immigration policies that expanded ICE operations nationwide. These policies resulted in broader enforcement activities, including raids in workplaces, businesses, neighborhoods, and public spaces. In communities with large immigrant populations such as Los Angeles, enforcement produced fear, uncertainty, and a decline in everyday participation in school, healthcare, and work.

Research shows that immigration detention and deportation extend beyond arrest records. They contribute to family separation, economic hardship, and psychological distress. While public narratives often emphasize Latinx experiences, this project focuses on UCLA Pilipinx students to demonstrate the broader effects across other immigrant communities.

Focus

Project focus

This project centers UCLA Pilipinx students whose family members, relatives, or close friends have experienced ICE detention or deportation. It documents how those incidents shape emotional well-being, financial stability, academic performance, and a student’s sense of belonging on campus.

By collecting and mapping anonymous testimonies, we aim to make visible the indirect consequences of enforcement—showing that the harm is shared by students who are connected to those detained, not only by the people who are removed.

Community

Why UCLA Pilipinx?

UCLA has a significant Pilipinx student community, and Samahang Pilipino is an important campus organization for cultural support and advocacy. By focusing on Pilipinx students, the project highlights how enforcement affects long-standing immigrant families and students who carry these experiences into the academic environment.

The project also challenges narrow public understanding of ICE enforcement by revealing that negative outcomes—such as anxiety, housing instability, and academic disruption—can span multiple racial and ethnic immigrant groups.

Methods

Community methods

We collect testimonies through an anonymous Google Form distributed to UCLA Pilipinx students via Samahang Pilipino, the UCLA Asian American Studies Center, and other campus channels. The survey asks respondents to describe who was affected, the type of relationship, their hometown state, and how ICE enforcement has influenced their family and student life.

The survey asks one main question: "Do you think UCLA has provided enough resources for students having experienced deportations?" Your answer and any comments are shared anonymously and appear on this map only as aggregated Yes/No counts and quotes.

Responses are gathered with respect for participant anonymity and designed to prioritize lived experience over statistical representation.

The map

What the map shows

The interactive map visualizes the location and distribution of testimonies from UCLA Pilipinx students. It emphasizes shared themes such as emotional distress, financial strain, educational disruption, and isolation in community spaces.

Instead of focusing on enforcement counts, the map helps reveal the recurring experiences that connect these stories and supports a narrative of collective impact.

Research

Academic context

Existing scholarship documents how deportation and forced separation cause long-term harms, including family fragmentation, economic insecurity, and mental health challenges. This project connects those findings to the UCLA Pilipinx student experience and aims to bring academic evidence into campus conversations.

References from national research provide a foundation for understanding why these testimonies matter and how they relate to broader community impacts of ICE enforcement.

Ethics

Limitations and ethics

Our survey is based on convenience sampling and voluntary participation, so it is not statistically representative of all Pilipinx students or all individuals affected by ICE enforcement. The data is intended to amplify lived experiences, not to produce generalizable findings.

The project is guided by ethical care for respondents, aiming to preserve anonymity while sharing stories that are often missing from official enforcement narratives.

Impact

Community impact

This project is designed to help affected students feel less isolated and to support Samahang Pilipino and campus organizations in understanding shared forms of harm. It creates a visual and narrative resource for community outreach, student advocacy, and mutual aid.

In the long term, the mapping tool may serve as a model for community-driven documentation of enforcement impacts across other immigrant student populations.

“As a UCLA Pilipinx student with loved ones affected by ICE, I want to share my story and see other testimonies so I can feel supported, validated, and part of a community working toward accountability and care.”
