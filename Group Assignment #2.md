## **Background**

In 2025, following President Donald Trump’s return to office, the federal government implemented a series of immigration policies that expanded Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) operations nationwide. These policies broadened ICE’s enforcement activities and led to large-scale raids in workplaces, businesses, neighborhoods, and other public spaces, resulting in widespread arrests and detention (Council on Foreign Relations, 2025). While the Trump administration argued that these operations were necessary to enforce federal immigration laws and detain individuals without legal authorization to remain in the United States (The White House, 2025), the effects extended beyond the individuals who were detained. Research has shown that immigration raids and deportation can contribute to family separation, economic hardship, and psychological distress (Langhout et al., 2018; Rojacs-Flores et al., 2020). ## **Objectives**

Our project aims to demonstrate how the impacts of ICE detentions extend beyond those directly affected who were either detained or deported. While the Latinx community has been disproportionately targeted, the after effects of ICE enforcements further impact families, friends, work place, schools, and communities as a whole. As our primary target organization includes the UCLA Samahang Pilipino community, our mapping application focuses on UCLA Filipinx students by collecting anonymous responses about whether someone close to them has been detained or deported by ICE and how that experience shaped their lives so far. Instead of solely focusing on the number of ICE detentions itself, our project plans to analyze the repercussions that follow: emotional distress, financial strain, physical and mental health, and everyday challenges. By visualizing these experiences in a mapping application, our group aims to help Samahang Pilipino identify shared recurring experiences, highlighting the consequences and challenges faced by communities affected by ICE enforcements. As prior research has found, "the broader community suffers negative consequences of deportation regardless of first-hand experience” (Langhout, Regina Day, et al).

## **Community methods**

This application is designed to empower immigrant communities broadly while directly supporting individuals whose lives have been affected by the detainment or deportation of a loved one. Although immigration enforcement is often portrayed as impacting only those who are directly detained, our project emphasizes that its consequences extend far beyond the individual, affecting the emotional well-being, financial stability, health, education, and daily lives of families, friends, and entire communities.   
Our primary target community consists of UCLA Samahang Pilipino community who have experienced the indirect effects of immigration enforcement through parents, siblings, extended family members, relatives, partners, or close friends. Through anonymous, community-generated testimonies, our mapping application will visualize the geographic distribution of the UCLA Pilipinx community who have experienced the indirect impacts of immigration enforcement to demonstrate the broadness of these impacts as well as to document the emotional and social consequences that are often absent from public discourse. We will collect testimonies that describe how the detention or deportation of a loved one has affected these students’ emotional well-being, financial stability, education, mental health, relationships, and everyday lives, as well as ask for their firsthand account of their experiences. By making these stories visible, we hope to validate these shared experiences, foster a sense of solidarity among those who may feel isolated in their struggles, and raise awareness of the widespread and interconnected effects of immigration enforcement.  
To collect these testimonies, we will distribute an anonymous Google Forms survey to UCLA Pilipinx students through campus organizations, such as Samahang Pilipino, the UCLA Asian American Studies Center, and on social media platforms. Because our survey relies on convenience sampling through Google Forms, a limitation of our survey is that responses may not be representative of all Pilipinx Americans or all individuals affected by ICE enforcement. Rather than produce statistically representative findings, our goal is to amplify lived experiences and illustrate the broader community impacts of ICE detention and deportation.

## **Long-term impact**

Although our initial version focuses on collecting anonymous testimonies from the UCLA Samahang Pilipino community, we envision our mapping application as a community-driven platform that can be adapted to serve immigrant communities more broadly in the future. By beginning with one specific community, we are able to document experiences in greater depth whilst creating a flexible framework that can later be expanded in collaboration with other immigrant communities.  
Throughout development, we hope to seek feedback from members of Samahang Pilipino to ensure the platform continues to reflect the priorities and lived experiences of the communities it serves rather than imposing outside assumptions about their needs. guidance on user privacy, approaches in collecting personal stories, and developing survey questions, data collection practices, and consent procedures to help encourage participants to comfortably and safely share their experiences. We plan to transfer ownership of our tool directly to one or more of these trusted community-based organizations so our tool can continue to evolve based on the needs and lived experiences of individuals directly and indirectly affected by immigration enforcement while remaining flexible and adaptable to each organization’s goals and community needs. As these organizations continue to develop the application, they may expand its use across different immigrant communities and geographic regions.   
By organizing community-generated testimonies into an interactive map, our platform aims to amplify lived experiences that are often absent from public discussions of immigration enforcement while helping organizations identify recurring challenges faced by affected communities. We hope our platform will become not only a repository of personal stories but also a resource for advocacy, community outreach, and public education by illustrating how the effects of immigration enforcement extend beyond those who are directly detained or deported. Ultimately, we hope to contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of these far-reaching impacts of immigration enforcement while fostering connection and solidarity among individuals with shared experiences.
